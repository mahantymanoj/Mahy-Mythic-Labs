# Preface

A photograph captures a moment.

A video captures a journey.

Motion creates emotion.

The movement of a camera, the expression of a character, the changing light of an environment, and the rhythm of editing all contribute to storytelling.

Artificial Intelligence has made cinematic video generation possible.

However, AI alone does not create films.

A filmmaker creates films.

The purpose of video direction is to transform individual AI-generated assets into meaningful cinematic sequences.

This handbook defines how Mahy Mythic Labs designs, generates, reviews, and assembles AI-generated video content.

The Video Director is responsible for:

* Shot design.
* Camera movement.
* Scene pacing.
* Motion decisions.
* Character actions.
* Environmental animation.
* Emotional progression.
* Continuity between shots.

The director answers:

"Why should the audience see this moment in motion?"

---

# Chapter 3 — The Mahy Mythic Labs Motion Philosophy

Every movement should follow three principles.

---

## 1. Purposeful Motion

Motion must communicate something.

Example:

Slow camera movement toward an ancient temple:

Communicates:

* Discovery.
* Respect.
* Wonder.

---

## 2. Natural Motion

Movement should feel physically believable.

Avoid:

* Unrealistic body movement.
* Random camera shaking.
* Artificial motion.

---

## 3. Emotional Motion

Motion should support the feeling of the scene.

Examples:

Slow movement:

* Mystery.
* Reflection.
* Spirituality.

Fast movement:

* Conflict.
* Action.
* Urgency.

# Book III — Camera Movement Language

---

# Chapter 7 — Camera Movement Types

## Push In

Camera moves closer.

Creates:

* Discovery.
* Importance.
* Emotional connection.

---

## Pull Out

Camera moves away.

Creates:

* Scale.
* Reflection.
* Ending moments.

---

## Pan

Camera moves horizontally.

Useful for:

* Landscapes.
* Architecture.
* Large environments.

---

## Tilt

Camera moves vertically.

Useful for:

* Tall structures.
* Mountains.
* Space objects.

---

## Orbit

Camera rotates around subject.

Useful for:

* Characters.
* Important objects.
* Dramatic reveals.

---

## Tracking Shot

Camera follows movement.

Useful for:

* Walking characters.
* Journeys.
* Exploration.

---

## Aerial / Drone Movement

Useful for:

* Cities.
* Nature.
* Cosmic scale.

---

# Chapter 8 — Camera Movement Rules

Avoid:

* Movement without purpose.
* Excessive camera motion.
* Constant zoom effects.
* Random transitions.

Professional cinema often uses controlled movement.

Less movement can create stronger impact.

# Chapter 14 — Video Quality Requirements

Approved video should have:

* Natural movement.
* Stable camera.
* Consistent characters.
* Realistic physics.
* Smooth transitions.
* Cinematic composition.

Avoid:

* Warped faces.
* Changing clothing.
* Object deformation.
* Unnatural motion.

---

# Chapter 15 — Scene Duration Guidelines

General guidance:

## Establishing Shot

5–10 seconds

Purpose:

Introduce world.

---

## Emotional Moment

4–8 seconds

Purpose:

Create connection.

---

## Information Shot

3–6 seconds

Purpose:

Explain concept.

---

## Action Shot

2–5 seconds

Purpose:

Create energy.
