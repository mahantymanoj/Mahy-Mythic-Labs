# Preface

An image is the smallest unit of cinematic storytelling.

Before movement exists, before narration begins, before music creates emotion, there is a single frame.

That frame has the power to communicate:

* Time.
* Place.
* Emotion.
* Scale.
* Culture.
* Meaning.

Artificial Intelligence has made image creation accessible.

However, generating an image is not the same as creating a meaningful visual.

A beautiful image without purpose is decoration.

A purposeful image becomes storytelling.

The Image Direction Handbook defines how Mahy Mythic Labs designs, generates, evaluates, and maintains cinematic AI images.

---

# Book I — The Philosophy of Image Direction

> "Do not create images. Create moments."

---

# Chapter 1 — The Purpose of Image Direction

Image direction transforms abstract ideas into visual experiences.

The purpose is to define:

* What the audience sees.
* Why they see it.
* How they should feel.
* What information the image communicates.

Every image should support the story.

---

# Chapter 2 — The Role of the Image Director

The Image Director is responsible for translating:

```
Research
    ↓
Story Meaning
    ↓
Visual Concept
    ↓
AI Prompt
    ↓
Final Image
```

The Image Director decides:

* Subject importance.
* Composition.
* Camera perspective.
* Lighting.
* Color.
* Atmosphere.
* Visual symbolism.

AI generates possibilities.

The director chooses the vision.

---

# Chapter 3 — The Mahy Mythic Labs Visual Standard

Every generated image should aim for:

## Cinematic Realism

Images should feel like frames captured from a high-quality documentary or feature film.

Avoid:

* Generic AI art appearance.
* Plastic textures.
* Unrealistic anatomy.
* Random fantasy elements.

---

## Story Purpose

Every image should answer:

Why does this frame exist?

Examples:

A temple at sunrise:

Not just architecture.

It communicates:

* Spirituality.
* Time.
* Peace.
* Human connection.

---

## Emotional Impact

The image should create a feeling.

Possible emotions:

* Wonder.
* Mystery.
* Reverence.
* Curiosity.
* Inspiration.
* Fear.
* Discovery.

# Chapter 5 — Visual Objective Document

Before writing any prompt, define:

```
Image Name:

Episode:

Purpose:

Story Moment:

Main Subject:

Emotion:

Information Communicated:

Reference Sources:

Required Elements:

Elements To Avoid:
```

This prevents random image generation.

---

# Chapter 6 — The Image Prompt Architecture

Every image prompt should contain:

```
1. Subject

2. Character/Environment Description

3. Historical or Contextual Setting

4. Action

5. Composition

6. Camera

7. Lighting

8. Mood

9. Visual Style

10. Technical Quality

11. Negative Constraints
```

---

# Chapter 7 — Subject Direction

The subject is the primary focus.

Define:

## Identity

Who or what is shown?

Examples:

* A Vedic sage.
* Ancient civilization.
* A distant galaxy.
* A historical scientist.

---

## Appearance

Define:

* Age.
* Clothing.
* Materials.
* Physical characteristics.
* Important details.

---

## Importance

Explain why the audience should notice this subject.

---

# Chapter 8 — Composition Rules

Composition controls viewer attention.

Mahy Mythic Labs uses:

## Rule of Thirds

Useful for:

* Characters.
* Landscapes.
* Documentary scenes.

---

## Symmetry

Useful for:

* Temples.
* Sacred architecture.
* Scientific structures.

---

## Center Composition

Useful for:

* Important symbolic moments.
* Divine or powerful subjects.
* Iconic visuals.

---

## Depth Composition

Use:

* Foreground.
* Middle ground.
* Background.

This creates cinematic depth.

---

# Chapter 12 — Image Quality Requirements

Approved images should have:

* Realistic anatomy.
* Natural textures.
* Correct lighting.
* Cinematic composition.
* High detail.
* Consistent style.

Avoid:

* Distorted faces.
* Extra fingers.
* Incorrect objects.
* Artificial expressions.

---

# Chapter 13 — Image Consistency System

For every episode maintain:

## Character Consistency

Same:

* Face.
* Clothing.
* Accessories.

---

## Environment Consistency

Same:

* Architecture.
* Geography.
* Lighting.

---

## Style Consistency

Same:

* Color grading.
* Cinematic style.
* Image quality.

---

# Chapter 14 — Image Review Checklist

Before approval:

## Story

* Does this image support the narrative?

## Accuracy

* Does it respect research?

## Composition

* Is the subject clear?

## Emotion

* Does it create the intended feeling?

## Quality

* Does it look cinematic?

## Continuity

* Does it match previous scenes?
