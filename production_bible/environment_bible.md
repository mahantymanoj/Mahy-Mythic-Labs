# Preface

Every great story exists within a world.

The world surrounding a character influences:

* Their emotions.
* Their decisions.
* Their culture.
* Their challenges.
* Their journey.

A mountain is not just a mountain.

A temple is not just architecture.

A battlefield is not just a location.

Every environment carries history, symbolism, emotion, and meaning.

For Mahy Mythic Labs, environments are not backgrounds.

They are characters within the story.

The Environment Bible defines the principles required to create realistic, cinematic, and consistent worlds across all productions.

# Book I — The Philosophy of Environment Design

> *"A world should not merely be seen. It should be experienced."*

---

# Chapter 1 — Purpose of the Environment Bible

The Environment Bible exists to establish standards for designing, documenting, and maintaining locations throughout Mahy Mythic Labs productions.

Its objectives are:

* Create believable worlds.
* Maintain visual continuity.
* Support storytelling.
* Respect historical and cultural context.
* Guide AI image and video generation.
* Build recognizable visual identity.

Every important location should have a documented identity before production begins.

---

# Chapter 2 — Environment Design Philosophy

Mahy Mythic Labs follows five principles:

---

## 1. Authenticity

Environments should reflect their:

* Historical period.
* Geographic location.
* Cultural context.
* Natural conditions.

A location should feel like it belongs to its world.

---

## 2. Story Function

Every environment should serve a narrative purpose.

Ask:

* Why does this scene happen here?
* What emotion should this location create?
* What information does this environment communicate?

---

## 3. Cinematic Beauty

Realism does not mean ordinary.

The goal is:

* Realistic materials.
* Natural lighting.
* Cinematic composition.
* Meaningful visual details.

---

## 4. Continuity

Recurring environments should maintain:

* Architecture.
* Geography.
* Lighting style.
* Color palette.
* Weather conditions.
* Time period.

---

## 5. Emotional Identity

Every environment should communicate an emotion.

Examples:

Mountain:

* Wisdom.
* Isolation.
* Spirituality.

Ancient city:

* Civilization.
* History.
* Human achievement.

Space:

* Wonder.
* Mystery.
* Scale.

# Chapter 5 — Geographic Design

Environment design begins with geography.

Document:

* Climate.
* Terrain.
* Elevation.
* Vegetation.
* Water sources.
* Natural resources.

Geography influences civilization.

A desert city should not look like a tropical kingdom.

A Himalayan monastery should not resemble a coastal temple.

# Chapter 13 — Common AI Environment Problems

Avoid:

---

## Random Architecture

Problem:

AI mixes multiple civilizations.

Solution:

Specify cultural and historical context.

---

## Unrealistic Scale

Problem:

Buildings lose realistic proportions.

Solution:

Define human scale references.

---

## Modern Elements in Ancient Settings

Problem:

AI adds incorrect objects.

Solution:

Use negative constraints.

---

## Generic Fantasy Appearance

Problem:

Environment loses authenticity.

Solution:

Ground imagination with research.

---

# Chapter 14 — Environment Review Checklist

Before approving a location:

* Is the geography realistic?
* Does architecture match the period?
* Does the environment support the story?
* Is the atmosphere emotionally appropriate?
* Does it maintain continuity?
* Does it avoid an artificial AI appearance?
* Does it respect cultural context?
