# Preface

Editing is the process of transforming individual creative elements into a complete storytelling experience.

A video is not simply:

* Images.
* Clips.
* Voice.
* Music.

A video is the relationship between these elements.

Editing determines:

* What the audience sees.
* When they see it.
* How long they experience it.
* What emotions they feel.

For Mahy Mythic Labs, editing is storytelling through time.

---

# Book I — The Philosophy of Editing

> "Editing is not cutting footage. Editing is controlling the audience's journey."

---

# Chapter 1 — The Purpose of Editing

Editing exists to:

* Create narrative flow.
* Maintain audience attention.
* Build emotional progression.
* Connect visuals and narration.
* Remove unnecessary information.
* Increase storytelling impact.

---

# Chapter 2 — The Editor's Responsibility

The editor must answer:

## What should the audience feel?

Before every cut.

---

## What information matters?

Remove unnecessary content.

---

## What deserves attention?

Guide viewer focus.

---

## What creates curiosity?

Build anticipation.

---

# Chapter 3 — Mahy Mythic Labs Editing Philosophy

Our editing follows five principles.

---

# 1. Story First

Beautiful visuals cannot replace a weak story.

The edit must always serve the narrative.

---

# 2. Rhythm

Every video has a natural rhythm.

Too slow:

Audience loses interest.

Too fast:

Audience cannot connect.

---

# 3. Clarity

The audience should always understand:

* Where they are.
* What is happening.
* Why it matters.

---

# 4. Emotion

Editing should enhance emotional moments.

Important moments deserve time.

---

# 5. Immersion

The audience should feel present inside the world.

# Chapter 8 — Hook Editing

The first 30 seconds are critical.

The edit should include:

* Strong visuals.
* Emotional music.
* Curiosity-driven narration.
* Fast understanding of the topic.

Avoid:

* Long introductions.
* Channel greetings.
* Slow openings.

# Book IV — Cutting Principles

---

# Chapter 10 — When To Cut

Cut when:

* The idea changes.
* The emotion changes.
* The location changes.
* New information begins.

Do not cut randomly.

---

# Chapter 11 — Shot Duration Guidelines

General recommendations:

## Establishing Shots

5-10 seconds

Purpose:

Create world awareness.

---

## Information Shots

3-6 seconds

Purpose:

Support explanation.

---

## Emotional Shots

5-12 seconds

Purpose:

Allow connection.

---

## Fast Sequences

1-3 seconds

Purpose:

Energy and excitement.

---

# Chapter 12 — Transition Philosophy

Transitions should support storytelling.

---

## Cut

Most powerful and natural.

Used for:

* Information flow.

---

## Fade

Used for:

* Time passage.
* Reflection.

---

## Dissolve

Used for:

* Connection between ideas.

---

## Camera Movement Transition

Used for:

* Cinematic continuity.

---

Avoid:

* Excessive effects.
* Flashy transitions.
* Distracting animations.


# Chapter 14 — Narration Editing

Rules:

* Remove unwanted pauses.
* Maintain natural breathing.
* Match visuals with words.
* Preserve emotional delivery.

---

# Chapter 15 — Music Mixing

Music should:

* Support emotion.
* Stay below narration.
* Increase during emotional peaks.
* Reduce during explanations.

---

# Chapter 16 — Sound Effects

Sound effects create realism.

Examples:

Temple:

* Bells.
* Footsteps.
* Wind.

Space:

* Ambient textures.
* Electronic sounds.

Nature:

* Birds.
* Water.
* Forest sounds.

# Chapter 18 — Visual Effects

Use effects to support storytelling.

Examples:

* Particle effects.
* Light rays.

# Chapter 19 — Subtitle Standards

Subtitles improve accessibility.

# Chapter 21 — Common Editing Mistakes

---

## Too Many Effects

Problem:

Viewer notices editing instead of story.

Solution:

Use effects intentionally.

---

## Slow Introduction

Problem:

Low retention.

Solution:

Start with curiosity.

---

## Poor Audio Balance

Problem:

Viewer leaves.

Solution:

Prioritize voice clarity.

---

## Random Visuals

Problem:

Beautiful but meaningless.

Solution:

Connect every shot to narration.
