# Stars

## Overview

Stars are massive spheres of plasma powered by nuclear fusion.

They produce:

* Light
* Heat
* Heavy elements essential for life

---

# Stellar Formation

Stars form inside molecular clouds and nebulae.

Gravity compresses gas and dust until the core temperature is high enough to ignite hydrogen fusion.

---

# Nuclear Fusion

Stars convert hydrogen into helium in their cores.

The energy released as light and heat is what we observe as starlight.

As a star ages it fuses progressively heavier elements depending on its mass.

---

# Star Lifecycle

## Main Sequence

The longest phase. A star burns hydrogen in a stable equilibrium between gravity and outward radiation pressure.

Our Sun is currently in this phase.

---

## Red Giant Phase

When hydrogen in the core is depleted, the star expands dramatically as it begins fusing hydrogen in outer shells.

---

## Supernovae

Massive stars (roughly eight or more times the mass of the Sun) end their lives in spectacular explosions called supernovae.

Supernovae forge and scatter elements including:

* Carbon
* Oxygen
* Silicon
* Iron (the heaviest element created by stellar fusion)

**Important accuracy note:** Elements heavier than iron — including gold, platinum, and uranium — are primarily created during neutron star mergers (r-process nucleosynthesis), not ordinary supernovae. This was confirmed by the detection of gravitational wave event GW170817 in 2017, which showed a neutron star merger producing heavy elements including gold. Some heavy elements may also form in rare types of core-collapse supernovae.

---

# Stellar Remnants

What remains after a star dies depends on its mass:

* **White dwarf** — remnant of a low-to-medium mass star like our Sun
* **Neutron star** — remnant of a massive star after a supernova; incredibly dense
* **Black hole** — remnant when a very massive star collapses beyond the neutron star limit

---

# Importance of Stars

All elements heavier than hydrogen and helium were created inside stars or during violent stellar events.

Carbon, oxygen, calcium, and iron in the human body were forged in stars that exploded long before our Solar System formed.

This is established scientific fact, not simply a poetic idea.
